Print & assembly guide

v5.5 — the Jetson is a press fit, and the ties are gone

  • The gap was 2.77 mm, not 1.5. Measured, not estimated — so 1.5 would have left 1.27 mm of slack and changed nothing. And the ceiling is not the whole camera head: it is a 16.8 mm band of it at y −21.1…−4.3. Everywhere else the kit has open sky.
  • The take-up is built UP FROM THE FLOOR, not down from the head — forced by the print, not a preference. The keel prints upright, so the head's underside faces the bed: any boss or rib hung off it starts its first layer in mid-air. The bay floor faces the other way. Same joint, same squeeze, only one of the two is printable. So the bay floor rises 2.4 mm solid and the kit presses up into the head; the reaction is the disc.
  • What it presses against — measured, and not what I assumed. The head's underside there is not flat and not the 55° chamfer: from y −19 to −11 it is the head plate's own face, tilted 8.6° by the eye axis (147.27 → 146.15), and the steep cut only starts past y −10. A flat pad parallel to the kit's top is not available and never can be — a horizontal face there is a 35 × 19 mm overhang, which is why that underside is angled. So the contact is a 35 mm line across the kit's top at y −11.5, opening to a 2.6 mm band at the working squeeze. That is the right answer, not a consolation: a line does not ask two rigid bodies to be parallel.
  • Four crush pads carry the entire compliance — 0.8 mm tall, on the raised floor at y ±28, under the kit's stiff ends. A rigid 0.33 mm squeeze between two rigid bodies is a coin toss: the keel's own z is accurate to a layer, but the kit's height is a number we do not control, and ±0.4 on it swings the joint from 0.7 mm of interference to 0.1 mm of rattle. The pads yield locally instead of jacking the kit against the head. They crush 41% of their height at nominal.
  • The zip ties are deleted — both structural ones, and their four slots with them. The press fit carries the kit now: pads below, head plate above, 36.4 mm of shoe either side. One tie is left in the whole build, on the camera flex, and it is strain relief. A tie that holds nothing is a hole in a stiffener — but if the press proves insufficient on a real kit, four cubes bring the slots straight back.
  • It goes in ANGLED, and it always did. Swept straight up the kit hits 3936 mm³ of camera head — all of it in that one band — so it could never have been lifted straight out, and no assembly step ever said so. Step 1 says it now. No entry relief is needed: rotating about its far end, a 0.2° tilt clears the squeeze. (I cut two reliefs first and measured them removing nothing — they are not in the part.)
  • Audit: keel ∩ jetson is no longer required to be zero — it carries a 20 mm³ budget and measures 16.0. Over is a clash, under is the press fit, and the number is printed either way. Measure your kit's height before printing: KIT_LIFT is the single constant that corrects it.
  • What did NOT move. PAN_TOP/PAN_BOT are decoupled from the kit now, so every member still roots at z39.1 into the disc — same welds, same 7530 mm² first layer, same 159.6 mm height. §6.5's rule is about the camera stack, and the kit is downstream of it: KIT_Z0 was always derived from the camera, never the other way round.
  • What DID move — the honest cost line: volume. The 2.4 mm solid floor rise took the keel from 94 to 102.8 cm³: ~9% more filament and print time on the part that already loses most of a day if it fails. That is the price of the press fit, and it is the one number in “same welds, same first layer, same height” that is not the same.

v5.4 — the keel and the floor are one part

  • Seven printed parts, not eight. The floor puck is gone as a part — it is the keel's own foot now. l6b1_floor is not exported any more; l6b1_keel carries the Ø99 disc, the three twist-lock lugs and the cradle as one solid.
  • Why it costs nothing to merge. The two already printed in the same orientation: the puck went bottom-face down, the keel goes upright on its pan, and the pan is parallel to the disc. Same axis, same down. The merged part prints exactly as the keel already did, standing on the disc instead of the pan.
  • And the joint never articulated. Sliding the keel onto the puck was assembly step 1; from then on nothing moved relative to it. A joint made once and never moved is a joint you can delete — so the 43.2 keel slot, the tab channel, its 45° ramp, the two hook blocks, the two detent bumps and the keel's two slide tabs are all gone, along with assembly step 1, the service step and the floor∩keel clash pair.
  • First layer 4097 → 7530 mm² (measured), and the disc reaches 28 mm forward of the pan's old front edge — which is the direction the mass leans. The brim is now belt-and-braces rather than the thing keeping it on the bed. 159.6 mm tall, 102.8 cm³, one body after the 0.2 mm erosion check, and the one support patch (head underside, print z108) is unchanged.
  • The kit's seat plane did not move. The bay floor is z40.3, exactly where the pan's top face was, so nothing above z40.4 changes by a layer (§6.5). Under the kit there is now 3.9 mm of solid where there was 2.7 + a 0.4 gap + 1.2 — same material, minus the gaps.
  • It closes a tolerance as well. The 0.4 mm/side slot fit is gone, and with it the 0.15 mm of lateral camera offset that fit allowed. The welds improve too: every member still runs 1.2 mm down through the bay floor into solid material (back plate alone, 386 mm³), and the −x land now stands 3.3 mm against the back plate's whole 92 mm outer face — a stiffener the split geometry could not have.
  • The bay is closed in y at ±47.5, not run through as the old slot was. The slide-in is gone, so the disc stays a continuous ring, and the end webs' outer faces land against retained full-height land.
  • What it costs, and it is real: a failed print now loses both parts — ~103 cm³ and most of a day, instead of just the keel. You also lose the option of reprinting one keel for a different dev kit and keeping the puck — free before, not free now.
  • It exposed a clash nobody had ever tested. Merging forced keel∩plugs into the matrix, and it reported 30.8 mm³: the +y end web has been standing 4.4 mm inside the barrel plug's path since v5 (plug reaches z54.4, the notch roof stopped at z50). v5 checked floor∩plugs and never keel∩plugs — absence from the matrix is indistinguishable from an untested pair. Fixed as a doorway: 17 wide × z36–56, cut open past the web's top rather than up to a 0.6 mm lintel over a 14 mm span, leaving two ~12.7 mm pillars that still stop the kit lengthwise. 17, not 14, because the old notch matched the plug's width exactly — 0.00 mm/side to a real plug on a real cable.
  • The lug-angle constraint relaxed and the angles did not. 90°→115° was forced by the slot and the tab channel, both of which are gone — but the spacing stays 100/90/170°, because keying the insertion orientation is a separate reason.
  • Audit: the bore check now measures the keel above the lug band (z>43.6) against r50 and the lugs on their own against the r52.1 arc slot — exempting the part outright, as v5 did with the puck, would now exempt the whole cradle. Everything the puck was checked against (kit, plug, camera, cable) is still checked, as part of the keel; that the two do not interfere is now answered by the weld check instead of a clash pair.

v5 — the keel has a bottom

  • What went wrong. The keel's base was four separate members — back plate, front wall, two end webs — and they met only where the Ø99 clip had already eaten their corners: 0.13 mm of contact at the back plate and 0.03 mm at the front wall. That is a connected mesh and a slicer that sees nothing, so it printed as four loose pieces. The old audit reported “1 piece” and was telling the truth.
  • Why it cannot be fixed at that height. The kit's 35 × 90.5 footprint puts its corners at r48.5 and the bore allows r49.5. Any closed loop around the kit has to squeeze through 0.98 mm minus clearance — thinner than two extrusions, wherever you put it, whatever shape it is. The tie has to go under the kit.
  • So the keel got a floor. A 1.2 mm pan at z39.1–40.3, spanning the whole base footprint, welded to all four members over ~700 mm² instead of 0.16. First layer goes from 754 → ~3800 mm², which also fixes the real risk with a 152 mm part whose mass leans forward.
  • And the puck paid for it, exactly. The kit pocket is deleted; the puck's top face drops 4.5 mm to z39.1 across a full-width slot, and the pan fills that slot. The kit still stands at z40.4 — the same plane it stood on in v4, which was the pocket floor. Nothing above z40.4 moves by a layer: board, lens, visor, shell, eye axis all untouched.
  • What is left of the puck is a 2.7 mm disc (z36.4–39.1 under the keel, full 7.2 mm at the rim for the lugs) plus two lands at x<-21.9 and x>21.0. The lands stiffen the disc into a channel section, carry all three lugs, and the -x land is the hook: the tabs run in a channel cut through it, so the separate hook blocks are gone (two short ones stay, to raise the land where the tabs come to rest so the channel's 45° roof traps 3.5 mm of tab).
  • One lug moved: 90° → 115°. The slot is open at both ends of y because the keel still arrives by sliding forward, so no lug may sit inside x -21.9…21.0 or across the tab channel at x -27.7…-20.7. At 90+18° it did both. Locked spacing is now 100/90/170° — still unique under rotation, and the entry channel is clear of the rear arch (it used to drop straight through it).
  • The audit now looks for this class of fault. decompose() counts bodies and can never see a 0.13 mm weld. So every part is now eroded 0.2 mm horizontally — intersected with itself nudged in eight compass directions — and its bodies re-counted: any neck narrower than one 0.4 mm extrusion in plan view falls apart, and nothing that is genuinely one wall does. v4's keel splits into four. Horizontal is the only direction that matters, because it is the one a nozzle cannot bridge.
  • Check before you print: the kit is 0.1 mm above the pan and its top corner still clears the camera head by 1.2 mm. Both numbers are unchanged, and both are in the clearance report.

v4 — the real camera module, and no fasteners

  • The platform was too small. The board is 37 × 37 and the lens is 26 mm long (Ø24 barrel, Ø17 clear aperture, 1.5 mm of screw focus beyond that). That fixes everything else: the board's back face — the seat plane — sits 37.4 mm behind the visor's outer face, and the barrel front is recessed 8.8 mm behind it (11.6 at the tile's lower rim, which the eye's 8.6° tilt pushes further out). Both figures are measured off the built tile, not off TILE_S — the constant says 3.5 where the surface it names is at 2.8. That recess is not styling, and it is not the board that sets it: the widest thing on the whole sub-assembly is the lens holder's own front rim (z166), because it reaches furthest toward the skin. Pulled 4 mm further forward it stood at r49.9 of the r50 bore — 0.1 mm from not going in at all. As built it is r46.1. The lens cannot come forward; the hole has to do the work instead.
  • Which is why the dev kit had to come down 11.6 mm. A 37 mm board reaching 18.5 mm below the eye axis lands exactly where the kit's top corner used to be (z155). v3 hid that by notching the platform 0.6 mm clear of the kit. Instead the floor puck went 10.8 → 6.4 mm thick and gained a 3.1 mm pocket, so the kit now stands at z40.4 and its top face is z143.4. Nothing is notched. The tightest point on the whole cradle is 0.65 mm (keel to kit, measured as a minimum gap over both solids) and 0.10 mm from the puck's pocket wall — measure your own kit before printing the puck.
  • The connectors are measured, and deliberately not fitted. Two connectors, both on the board's back face: the black 3-row block (14.0 × 10.3, 2.9–14.1 mm down from the top edge) and the white 4-pin (7.3 × 3.6, near the bottom edge). Measured to ±0.15 mm — which is exactly why neither gets a pocket shaped to it. A frame that close to a photogrammetric number fails if the measurement is off, if the tolerance stacks the wrong way, or if the board is handed the other way round. Instead the head has one 32 × 36.8 opening straight through the 9 mm plate: connectors, heatsink and both cables all pass rearward through it with ≥5.2 mm of side clearance and ≥1.3 mm top and bottom. The bridge and the old 12 × 7 cable slot are deleted, and the clamp's escape notch with them.
  • No screws, no heat-set inserts, anywhere. All four M3 inserts and eight screws are deleted. The keel has two tabs that slide under two hooks on the puck and click over a detent; the camera board slides straight into a three-sided tray from the front — the channel lips used to run its full height and leave a 32.2 mm gap for a 37 mm board, so they have moved above the board's top edge and nothing overhangs its path — and it is held by a 2.5 mm staple \u2014 two arms and a top bridge, open at the bottom \u2014 that drops down two channels and snaps past two lips. A closed ring could never be slid onto a lens standing 26 mm proud of the board, and nor could a yoke open at the top: whatever sits below the hole has to travel through the barrel. Only a part with nothing below the barrel makes the trip. Bill of materials: three zip ties and one drop of glue for the visor.
  • How the Ø24 lens is guaranteed to fit — three separate checks. (1) Inside the egg: every part of the sub-assembly is measured as a radius about the body axis and must be ≤ r50. (2) Rotation: because that test is a radius and not a footprint, anything that passes it also sweeps freely through the 18° twist — there is no orientation in which it is wider. (3) Through the aperture: the visor's hole is a 29.2 × 26.4 capsule, sized off the Ø17 optic and the 100° field, not off the barrel. Measured honestly the glass sits 8.8 mm back from the tile's face (11.6 at its lower rim), so the field itself is well clear — at the worst rim it needs 22.1 × 16.6, and it has 4.9 mm of margin — but the full Ø17 pupil would want 39.1 × 33.6, which no hole in a 33 mm tile can give. The corners therefore lose light rather than image: ray-traced pupil throughput at the 100° corners is 63% bottom, 91% top — about 0.7 EV, on the order of a wide lens's own falloff. Live margins: 3.9 mm inside the bore radius, 0.9 mm between the staple's arms and the Ø26 holder over the whole slide, and 0.2 mm between the visor's sleeve and that holder top and bottom — the audit prints the first two under “camera fit”, so a lens change that breaks one cannot pass silently. The 0.2 is against the Ø26 envelope: a knurled focus ring wider than Ø26 fouls the sleeve.
  • Rack the focus IN before insertion. The lens is 26 mm long with a Ø24 barrel and a Ø17 clear aperture, and it sits 8.8 mm behind the visor's hole. Racked fully OUT the barrel reaches r47.4 at z166 — 2.6 mm inside the r50 bore, so it passes the bore either way; but the bore is not the constraint. A swept-volume check of the 18° twist finds 7.5 mm³ of the racked-out barrel against the shell's thickened eye pad (x −22…−8.9, z 181.5–185.2), because the shell's inner radius there is 49.0, not 50. Racked in, the tip sits 1.5 mm further back and the twist is clear. The focus travel is still modelled as its own volume and the audit still prints its radius: if the lens ever gets longer, the “rack it in” step comes back automatically instead of being discovered on the bench.

v3 — the kit is 103 × 90 × 35

  • The board could not go in, and could not have fitted anyway. I had been building to NVIDIA's bare 100 × 79 × 21. At 103 × 90 × 35 the kit standing on edge presents a 90 × 35 cross-section — a half-diagonal of 48.3 mm (the cradle and the audit are still built to 90.5, deliberately: build to the larger number) — so it needs a Ø100 bore, against the Ø82.8 it had; and the Ø110 egg only cleared r48.5 between z58 and z156, a 97 mm band for a part that stands 103 tall.
  • The egg is Ø130. Same profile family, same 190 mm height, 18% fuller: rim r53.75, Ø100 bore (2–3 mm around the kit at every height it occupies), and a 2.4 mm flat rim to stand on. Silhouette, vents, twist-lock, hat, badge and antenna port all carried over and rescaled.
  • The keel opened up with it: 36.4 mm shoe interior for the 35 mm kit, a 92 mm back plate, the end lip at y-46.6, and the whole cradle still hard-clipped to Ø99 so it cannot exceed the hole it passes through.
  • The eye is solved, not hardcoded. The face of a fatter egg is further out, so the eye's centre, its depth under the skin and every pocket/tile scale factor are now derived from the profile at runtime. The camera stack sits 5 mm back down the eye axis to stay inside the bore, and the aperture opened to Ø19 to keep the view clear.

Whimsy

  • The antenna leans. Its bayonet port sits 8 mm off the axis, so the stem comes out of the dome's shoulder rather than its apex — and the crown itself stays a closed, uninterrupted dome. Quarter-turn in, straight out for travel.

The body

  • No arms. The flanks are clean again — both shoulder pads and their Ø4.3 bores are gone, and the three gills per side are back to full length.
  • No legs. The egg sits flat on the desk on its own 2.4 mm rim; the charcoal keel's base disc is hidden up inside the bottom opening.
  • Crown. The dome is closed — no flat, no port through the apex. The bayonet socket is 8 mm off the axis, so the Ø6.6 bore opens as an ellipse in the dome's shoulder and the antenna leans out of it. Putting it on the axis meant truncating the crown at z225 to a Ø12.5 flat, which is a hole in the top of the egg that only a hat can hide.
  • Eye. A charcoal capsule visor — 63 mm nominal across the face, a 56 × 33 mm part, set into a 2.4 mm pocket, its outer face 0.3 mm under the skin so it can never stand proud. It is FLUSH to the pocket wall bar 0.2 mm of glue clearance — the shadow line is the recess, not a groove. Behind the aperture the tile carries a capsule sleeve — the 29.2 × 26.4 aperture plus a 1.0 mm wall, merged into the tile's own outer surface and running back to s−17 — and the shell's bore is a matching capsule 0.3 mm clear of it. The tunnel is charcoal end to end: reaching the white step needs a ray 53.6° off axis, by which angle the tile's own rim occludes first. No lit internals and no white shell visible through the eye, from any angle. Gaze is 8.6° up.
  • Why the rim is clean now. Three separate faults, each hiding the next. The pocket cutter was bounded by the same revolve the shell's own skin comes from, so the cut arrived carrying a face coincident with the face it was cutting. The outer profile was sampled every 2.5 mm, and a cut THROUGH a surface exposes its polyline where shading normally hides it — 0.79 mm now, in 256 steps. And the last of it was not solid at all: three.js was averaging one normal across a 60° corner at the rim, and because a revolve alternates which rim vertices carry a wall triangle, the blend alternated too and lit up as a comb of teeth. Normals come from Manifold with a 40° sharp-edge threshold. The exported mesh was always watertight.

Airflow

  • Intake: 3 slots per side low on the waist (z≈64) — the bottom is sealed by the desk.
  • Mid + exhaust: 3 gills per side at z≈128, 3 slots high on the rear behind the dome. Both side rows now share one set of angles (±14° off the flank), so they stack into three clean columns instead of a 14° row over an 18° one. Fan → side plenum → chimney.

Hardware review — what it fixed

  • The top of the sub-assembly would not go through the hole. Everything is lowered up through the Ø82.8 bore, so nothing may exceed r41.4 about the body axis — but the camera's lens barrel reached r42.15 at z170. The whole camera stack (keel head, plate, standoffs, board, lens, heatsink pocket) now sits 2 mm further back down the eye axis: widest point r40.2, and the lens is still inside the visor's light-tight collar, so the view is unchanged. The audit measures this on every part now, so it cannot come back.
  • The bottom opening. Ø100 bore, r53.75 rim: 2 mm around the kit's 48.5 mm half-diagonal, and the keel is hard-clipped to Ø99 so nothing can exceed the hole it passes through.
  • Kit clearance: 36.4 mm shoe for a 35 mm kit, 92 mm between the two end webs for a 90 mm kit. It stood at z40.4 from v4 through v5.4 \u2014 the puck's pocket floor, then the keel's pan, then the merged bay floor, always the same plane. v5.5 raises it to z43.5 on four crush pads so it presses into the camera head: the first deliberate move of that seat, and the only thing that moves with it is the kit.
  • Turn slot was blind. The flat 22 mm cube only reached the bore at its centre, so the lug jammed 2 mm in; it is a swept arc now, and the twist is 18° (13° never pushed the lug clear of its 9 mm entry channel).
  • Lock wall. The old slots left a 0.6 mm skin on the outside of a 2 mm rim. Now 2 mm of skin outside a 4 mm band.
  • Inserts and screws: gone (v4). Two tabs run in a channel through the puck's own land and click over a detent; a snap-in clamp holds the board. Nothing to melt in, nothing to strip.
  • Camera: 37 × 37 board, 1.4–1.8 mm thick (1.6 nominal), slid in from the front along the eye axis into a 38.0 mm pocket, landing on a tapered ledge and held flat by a snap-in clamp — no standoffs, no hole grid to match, so it fits any 37 mm module of this family. It is not a drop-in-from-above tray: the heatsink and both cables are on the back face and would have to pass through 9 mm of head plate.
  • Feet. The rim is 2.4 mm and chamfered 0.6 — a Ø106 ring of first layer; it stands on itself; add a felt ring if it must not slide.

Cables — there are only two

  • Power (Ø7). Barrel plug onto the kit's rear face, then straight out the rear arch. Two things were in its way: the shoe's rear end lip (deleted — the front lip and the press fit locate the kit) and the floor puck, which blocked the arch down to z48. The disc now has a 22 mm scallop at the arch. v5.5: the lead leaves ~7 mm above the desk, not ~4 — the kit rose 3.1 mm for the press fit and its power socket rose with it. The rim slot is open through the rim, so the cable still turns down and lies flat.
  • Camera cables. Both plug into the back of the board and leave straight back through the head's opening — nothing to thread, nothing to align — then outboard of the keel tongue and down the +x flank to the kit's CSI header. One tie slot in the shoe's front wall takes the strain.
  • Both are modelled as swept volumes and included in the clearance audit, so they cannot silently be designed out again.

Camera heatsink

  • 26 mm sink at the back of the board, hanging through the head's one 32 × 33.0 opening (the same opening the connectors and cables use — a 30 × 30 sink passes with 1.0 at the sides and 1.5 top and bottom) into the keel's own chimney, so the three rear exhaust slots draw across it rather than past a sealed box. The blind pocket is gone.
  • It also means the seat plane is the head's own face — the board is supported at its rim by the tray, not floated on standoffs over a cavity.

Parts / plates (P1S, no AMS)

  • White plate: body shell, rim-down, no supports. Brim it — 190 mm tall on a 2.4 mm-wide rim.
  • Charcoal plate: keel cradle (upright on its own base disc — v5.4 merged the floor puck into it, so the first layer is the Ø99 disc, ~7500 mm², and every wall above it is vertical or knifed), camera clamp (flat, pads UP — the 2.8 mm plate face goes on the bed; pads down leaves 212 mm² of plate 0.6 mm in the air), eye visor (face-up), antenna (upright).
  • Red plate: the antenna's ball tip, flat-side-DOWN — a Ø7.1 mm first layer with a Ø4.15 hole in it, no supports. One part, a couple of minutes; it is the only red on the robot.
  • Brim the visor, the antenna and the keel — visor and antenna land on 81 and 26 mm² (measured off the built solids), and the keel now lands its whole base disc — ~7500 mm² (v5's pan was 4097; v4's narrow strips, 754). Brim it anyway: it is 159.6 mm tall and its mass leans forward over the camera head — though the disc reaches 28 mm forward of the old pan edge, so it is far less of a lever than it was.
  • Supports: one patch, one part — the keel. The underside of the camera head — 279 mm² at print z108 (x −14…25, y −20…−11), the 9 mm-thick plate's bottom edge. The rear underside is knifed off at 55° in the printer's frame, but the front cannot be: the space under the head is where the dev kit's top 25 mm lives, so there is nowhere to put a gusset. It is not a skim patch either — it is the foundation of the window's +x pillar, 7 × 15 mm in section and free-standing for 40 mm until it rejoins the keel at z143.5. Use a dense support interface. The roof of the 32 × 33.0 connector window is not a support patch: it is a 32 mm bridge anchored both sides. But it is tilted 8.6°, and slicers often miss sloped bridges and print them as overhang — check the preview, and if it is flagged, support that roof too. Droop there eats into J2's top clearance, which is 1.8 mm — the roof is relieved 1.0 mm over J2 (dx ±9.1) for exactly this reason. The 2.75 mm this line used to quote was the old 36.8 mm window's number; at 33.0 with no relief it was 0.8. The audit computes and prints every connector's clearance now, so it cannot go stale again. Both areas are buried inside the body and never seen. Every other part, including the shell, prints support-free.
  • Why the keel stands up: laid on its back it rested on one corner of the camera head — 34 mm² of first layer and 8400 mm² of the plate hanging 6.5 mm in mid-air. Upright it starts on the base disc's ~7500 mm², and the brim is for the leaning mass, not the area.
  • Why the visor is face-up: it is a curved tile, so either way it lands on a line. Face-up puts the drooping side on the glue face and the good surface — the robot's face — on top.

Settings

  • PLA, 0.2 mm layers, 0.4 mm nozzle, 3 walls, 15% infill. Supports only on the keel's z108 head-underside patch (dense interface); brim the shell, the keel, the visor, the antenna and the badge.
  • No per-part overrides — every part prints on the same profile. The one that used to need one is the visor: its sleeve behind the aperture was a 0.70 mm wall over the 11 mm the lens holder occupies, which is one 0.4 mm line plus gap fill, so it carried a “print l6b1_eye_visor at 0.35 mm line width, 2 walls” override — unapplicable the moment the visor arrives as one object among eight in an imported 3MF. The relief behind s-6 is 0.2 mm instead of 0.3 now, so the wall is 0.80 mm = 2 × 0.40 at the default line width. It is paid for out of clearance that was never as tight as the old note claimed: the built gap over the Ø26 holder measured 0.50 mm, not the 0.19 the text carried, and it is 0.40 mm now (0.25 with the keel at the far end of its ±0.15 mm du float). The audit measures the wall and the gap off the built solid every run.

Hardware

  • No screws and no heat-set inserts. One zip tie and a drop of glue for the visor — that is the whole bill of materials. The kit's two structural ties are gone (v5.5: it is a press fit — crush pads below, head plate above); the one tie left is strain relief on the camera flex. Keel→puck no longer exists (v5.4: one part), board→keel is a snap-in clamp, the antenna is a bayonet, the hat is a clearance socket that drops over the crown (0.15 mm at its rim, 0.30 above z207 — no stem, no catch), and shell→keel is the twist-lock.

Assembly

The order matters. Three steps used to be in the wrong place, each of them provably impossible: the visor cannot go in before the sub-assembly, the barrel plug cannot go on before the twist, and the focus cannot be racked out during it. All three were caught by sweeping the motions as volumes, not by looking at the assembled state.

  1. v5.4: there is no step 1 any more. The keel arrives with its floor on it — no slot to line up, no tabs to cam over a detent, nothing to pull-test. Start with the dev kit.
  2. Stand the dev kit on edge in the keel's bay, ports toward the rear arch, power end down. It goes in ANGLED — one end down first, then swing the other end down; it cannot be lowered in flat, and it never could (swept straight up it hits 3936 mm³ of camera head). Then press it home: it is a press fit now — four crush pads under it, the camera head's underside bearing on a 35 mm line across its top at y −11.5. Firm and square, and it stays put with no ties. The two end webs, rooted in the base, stop it lengthwise. Leave the barrel plug off — it does not clear the shell at the insertion angle (step 4).
  3. Camera — it goes in from the FRONT, along the eye axis. Feed both camera cables back through the head's 32 × 33 opening first, then slide the board straight back into the tray: heatsink and connectors into the opening, lens forward, board bottom landing on the tapered ledge (0.1 mm of drop, 0.5 mm of side play). It stops when its back face lands on the seat frame — the ring of head plate around the opening, 2.5 mm down each side and 2.0 top and bottom. There is no path from above: the heatsink and the cables stand off the back face and the head plate is 9 mm of solid material either side of the window. Nothing retains it until the clamp lands, so hold it with a finger. Then the clamp: lens through its open bottom, arms down the two channels, push until both barbs click into the rail pockets (~4 N, one audible click each). Its two 2 mm pads land on the board's outer border, directly over the seat strips behind it — the plate itself never touches the board. One zip tie on the flex at the shoe.
  4. Rack the focus fully IN. Racked out, the barrel fouls the shell's eye pad during the twist in step 5 (7.5 mm³, at z181.5–185.2). Racked in it is clear. You will set the focus properly in step 7.
  5. Stand the shell rim-up and lower the whole sub-assembly in through the bottom: the keel's three lugs drop down the entry channels, then twist 18° — to the mark, not “until it stops”, since the lock free-runs ~1.9° past its position. Lug spacing is uneven — it only locks eye-forward.
  6. Now fit the barrel plug, reaching in through the Ø100 bore, and lay the lead out through the rear slot. v5.3: the slot is open all the way down through the rim, so the cable leaves at desk level and lies flat instead of being led over the 4 mm ring that used to close the arch.
  7. Reach in through the bottom and set the focus, out to as far as 27.5 mm. Racked out it stays inside r50, so nothing has to come apart to do this.
  8. Glue the visor in last. Press it straight into the eye pocket along the eye axis — its sleeve slides down over the lens holder with 0.2 mm top and bottom, and that is the only direction that works: the sleeve captures the holder, so there is no path that fits the visor first and then lifts the sub-assembly past it (312–363 mm³ of overlap on every route tried). It follows that the bond has to be broken to get the sub-assembly out again — see Service.
  9. Antenna: drop it into the crown port and quarter-turn. It pulls straight out for travel — the body then packs as a plain egg.
  10. Ball tip onto the antenna: press the red sphere down over the stem's Ø4 pin until its flat underside lands on the shoulder at z24.5 — 0.15 mm of fit over 4 mm of engagement. Friction only; if your printer runs tight, ream the socket rather than force the ball.
  11. Crown fitting: the antenna. Its bayonet port sits 8 mm off the axis, so the stem leans out of the dome's shoulder and the crown itself stays a closed dome. Quarter-turn in, straight out for travel.

Service

  • The visor is the one destructive step. Its sleeve sits over the lens holder, so the sub-assembly cannot come out until the tile is prised out of its pocket and the glue bond broken. Use one drop, at the pocket wall, not on the sleeve — and expect to reprint the visor if you ever open the robot. Everything downstream of it is tool-free.
  • Break the visor bond, then twist the shell off; rack the focus in; the sub-assembly lifts out through the Ø100 bore. The Jetson tilts out — lift one end to break the press fit, then swing it clear; it will not come out flat.
  • The camera board comes out by prying the clamp up at its notch — the notch is mid-bridge and the barbs are 18 mm out at the arms' free ends, so the middle lifts first and the two barbs cam out of their pockets on their 34° release ramps. Firm, two hands, no tool sharper than a screwdriver. The board then slides straight out along the eye axis.
  • Nothing else comes apart, and nothing else needs to: the keel is the floor now, so the last teardown step is tilting the Jetson out. Nothing but the visor is glued, and there is no fastener to lose.

Fit notes

  • The audit intersects every part pairwise (+ Jetson / plug / camera / cable ghost volumes) — all must be zero except keel ∩ jetson, which carries a 20 mm³ press-fit budget (v5.5) and is reported as an intended interference. It also checks that every part of the sub-assembly fits inside the Ø100 bore it has to pass through, and slices each part in its print orientation to catch empty layers, loose bodies, first-layer area, and any region a layer below does not support — telling bridges apart from cantilevers, and naming where the worst one is. It yields between parts, so it reports progressively instead of freezing the page.
  • What the audit does not check. Its overhang scan is sampled — 80 probes, so ~1.9 mm apart on the keel — and it misses any patch shorter than that. Clearances are otherwise reported for the assembled state, with three exceptions that are now swept or tested as volumes on every run: the board's slide-in along the eye axis, the clamp's drop down its channel, and a 1.1 mm-tall envelope over the board's whole front face (everything except the outer 2 mm border and the holder) against the clamp — that last one is what proves the plate bridges the components rather than pressing them. The other three motion faults (visor before sub-assembly, plug before twist, focus racked out during it) were found the same way by hand — re-sweep if you move anything.
  • Two hard limits on the camera module — check them with calipers before you commit. (1) The barrel may project at most 27.0 mm from the board's front face racked fully IN (nominal 26). At 27.5 the racked-in barrel fouls the shell's eye pad during the insertion twist (6.8 mm³ at z181.5–185.2) and the sub-assembly cannot be turned into its lock. The r50 bore is not the limit — that is 29.8 mm — and the barrel can never touch the visor, since Ø24.6 passes straight through a 29.2 × 26.4 hole. (2) Nothing over the barrel may exceed Ø26.3 — a knurled focus ring, a locking screw or a retaining nut wider than that fouls the visor's sleeve, which is the last part fitted and is glued. The staple is not the stop; it admits Ø29.0.
  • The clamp, and what actually holds the board. Front-loaded, the board is pinched between two things: behind it, the seat frame — the ring of head plate around the connector window, 2.5 mm down each side, 2.0 top and bottom (the window was 36.8 tall, which left 0.1 mm of seat at top and bottom; it is 33.0 now, with a local 23 mm notch for J1's 4-pin, which reaches 0.6 mm lower). In front of it, the clamp — the flat charcoal staple, sliding down the two channels in the tray walls after the board is in, never before and never into the board's own 1.5 mm. Rails take the sides, the ledge the bottom, the lips the top. The clamp touches the board only on two 2.0 mm pads at dx 16.4–18.4, aligned with the seat strips behind, in the outer 2 mm border where no module of this family puts a component; the 2.8 mm plate itself stands 1.3 mm clear of the front face (1.1 on a thick board) on those pads, so it bridges over anything soldered inboard instead of sitting on it. Retention is two sprung barbs on the clamp's own arms dropping into pockets in the rail faces: 0.6 mm of deflection, ~4 N, one click each. The channel is 5.9 mm deep to take board 1.4–1.8 + pads 1.6 + plate 2.8 + 0.2 float.
  • Cables exit through the rear slot, which is open through the rim — the lead turns down and lies flat on the desk (it leaves the socket ~7 mm up since v5.5). It costs 16 mm of the rim's ~338 mm circumference: first layer 755 mm², down from 796, and the shell stands on the rest. The audit checks that everything on the sub-assembly stays inside the Ø100 bore.
  • Prefer the 3MF. It carries millimetres and real topology.
  • Ghosts travel with their datum (v5.5). The power plug and the Ø7 lead were z literals, written when the kit's seat was frozen at 40.4. Raising the kit 3.1 mm moved its socket and left both ghosts behind, so keel∩plugs, shell∩plugs, keel∩cable and shell∩cable were all still reporting green — evaluated 3.1 mm below where the hardware now is. §7.15 in its nastiest shape yet: not a pair missing from the matrix, but a pair in the matrix evaluated in the wrong place. It reads identically either way, and it would have kept lying the moment the bench retunes KIT_LIFT — which the audit itself tells them to do. All three kit-referenced ghosts are derived off KIT_Z0 now — the camera flex's CSI-header end was missed on the first sweep, one comment away from the two that were fixed, which is the rule earning a second hearing. The doorway was re-sized off the moved ghost too (z36–60: the plug's top had gone 1.5 mm above the old roof).
